How the wood grows

The forest is not stored anywhere. It is worked out, afresh, from one number. This page shows the rules, and every picture on it is drawn live by the same arithmetic the forest uses.

1 · SEEDA seed is a place

Everything starts from a function called hash(x, z, k). Give it two whole numbers and a channel, and it scrambles them with the seed into a fraction between 0 and 1. The same inputs always give the same answer, so nothing has to be remembered: ask about a spot twice, a year apart, and the wood says the same thing.

Change the seed here and every figure on the page redraws as a different wood.

2 · FIELDSFour maps are the whole ecology

The wood has no hand-placed anything. It has four fields, each a formula over noise, plus the height of the ground. Every plant and animal reads these and nothing else.

3 · SCATTEROne question per square

To place trees, the ground is ruled into 5 m squares. For each square the wood asks its rule one question: does something grow here, and what? The rule rolls its hash dice against the fields at that spot. A yes gets a position jittered inside the square, a species and a size.

Because the dice are the hash of the square's own coordinates, the answer never changes. The wood only ever works out the squares near you, and when you walk back, the same trees are standing there.

5 · PRIMITIVESFive shapes, four verbs

Blender supplies five plain meshes and nothing else that has a shape. In the browser, four operations bend them into every form in the wood.

6 · GROWTHOne recursion grows every woody thing

A trunk is swept upward. Along it, children branch off at a pitch, each a shorter, thinner copy of the same act, and so on down. Where the recursion stops, leaf cards are hung. There is one function for this, and a species is only a row of numbers handed to it.

Every individual also jitters its own spread, crown height and leaf size, so no two trees share a shape. The wood keeps four individuals of each species and scatters those.

The leaves themselves are the one thing that is modelled, not computed: Blender builds four small sprigs (broadleaf, conifer, fern, leaf cluster), photographs them into a single image with a matching relief map, and each card shows one of the four.

7 · SURFACESWhat Blender bakes

One script, run once, writes every asset. Besides the five primitives and the sprig image it makes three tiling surfaces. Bark and rock are noise shaped in frequency space, which wraps perfectly at the edges. The forest floor is a real bed of several thousand modelled leaves, needles and twigs, rendered from above.

8 · LIGHTSun, air and lens

A clock drives everything about light. The sun rises at six and sets at eighteen; the moon rides exactly opposite it. From the sun's height the clock sets the light's colour and strength, the sky, the mist and the exposure. Showers drift through on their own slow noise, closing the cloud and dimming the sun.

Each frame is then finished in five steps.

Wind, wingbeats and the glow of sun through a leaf are small additions to the surface shaders, shared by every plant.

The wood is about 670 lines of JavaScript on top of three.js, plus one Blender script. The page it loads is about 2.2 MB. This document carries its own copy of the noise, field, scatter and growth rules, so it stands alone.
